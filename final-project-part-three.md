# The final data story

View the final data story on Shorthand:
https://carnegiemellon.shorthandstories.com/can-a-coral-reef-recover-twice/index.html

My final project follows changes in living coral cover and reef fish at two monitoring sites on the northern coast of Moorea, French Polynesia. Through Tableau charts and animated scenes created in Blender, it shows a substantial recovery after an initial collapse, followed by another period of decline.

# Changes made since Part II

Since Part II, I completed the Shorthand narrative and the four animated reef scenes. These scenes illustrate the baseline, the first collapse, the recovery, and the later decline. They provide a visual introduction to the changes explored in the charts. They are conceptual illustrations rather than exact reconstructions of the monitoring sites, and if I had more time to work, I would like to polish the scene to match real site even better.

I also added context about the disturbances and the conditions left behind. Feedback from classmate suggested that the story needed more explanation of why the changes occurred. In response, I included research on how retained dead coral skeletons can affect grazing, algae, and the establishment of new corals. I presented this as a possible explanation for slower recovery, rather than a cause established by my charts.

Another suggestion is showing more different fish species to make the story more engaging. I incorporated fish imagery and used feeding groups to explain differences within the fish community. Following the ETC Game Design student’s feedback, I revised the ending to provide a clearer conclusion and added a link to Coral Gardeners as a way for readers to learn about restoration and support ongoing work.

I finally changed the title from “Can a Coral Reef Recover Twice?” to “A Recovery That Didn’t Last.”  because I think the story is more about reflecting the changes shown in the record, not giving an definite answer on whether the reef can recover or not. 

## The audience

The intended audience is people who are interested in marine biology or just biology in general, who might have limited idea on what a lot of coral reef is going through with the changing climate/ecology. They may be familiar with bleaching but less familiar with details on coral cover, coral composition, or fish feeding groups.

To make the story accessible, I explained coral cover and fish density in plain language and introduced technical terms near the relevant charts. The goal is to introduce the problem, using data visualization to show the problem clearly, and call out to the viewers for some action. The narrative begins with the location and an illustrated overview before moving into the data. Fish imagery helps readers connect unfamiliar group labels with recognizable animals.

## Final design decisions


I organized the story from the condition on coral reef itself with overall coral-cover trend to coral composition chart, then to surrounding ecosystem with total fish density, feeding groups, and corallivores charts. I think this sequence lets readers understand the main change before exploring differences within the ecosystem.

I used line charts because the main question concerns change over time. Keeping the two monitoring sites visible allows readers to compare their patterns. The Blender scenes add a sense of reef structure and appearance, while the charts communicate the measured changes.

An important lesson was to distinguish what the data show from what they can explain. Similar trends in coral cover and fish density do not establish that coral loss alone caused changes in fish populations. I also revised the wording to preserve that distinction and used published research to provide context.

# Final thoughts

Finally, This project helped me understand how much interpretation is needed between EDA and communicating a story. The total coral-cover trend provided the starting point, but examining coral composition and different fish groups made the account more specific. If there are no clear correlation between the fish group density data to the coal data, then the story wouldn't have worked. So it was interesting to clean the data, and explore with visualization to see if there is a story, and if so, what direction does it lead? 

I was excited about experimenting with 3d render animation in a presentation, which I was always interested in doing, and I had the excuse to do it in this project!
